Featured Case Study

How Can a Wellness Technology Company Play It Smart?

Analyzing Fitbit Consumer Data to Unlock Growth Avenues for Bellabeat

Data Source: CC0 Public Fitbit Survey (Kaggle)

Case Study Outline

Cleaned Rows: Excludes Non-Worn

Filtered out 61 records where steps were exactly 0 and sedentary time was 1440 minutes, ensuring high accuracy.

1. Topic & Objective

As part of the capstone project, this case study explores smart device usage trends from non-Bellabeat users. The primary research objective is to analyze public FitBit Fitness Tracker Data and model behavioral patterns to unlock new avenues of growth for Bellabeat in the global women's wellness market.

The Business Problem

How can Bellabeat play it smart and use competitor telemetry trends to refine and optimize its device features, application subscription models, and brand marketing?

2. Data Source & Integrity

The dataset consists of fitness telemetry from 30+ consenting Fitbit users surveyed via Amazon Mechanical Turk, uploaded to Kaggle by Mobius. It relationally maps minute-by-minute, hourly, and daily metrics across multiple physical states.

Data Quality Audit (ROCCC Framework)

Dimension Rating Strategic Action / Mitigation
Reliability Low-Moderate Small sample size (33 active, 24 sleep users). Identified and filtered out inactive device logs.
Originality Low (Third-Party) Sourced via distributed mechanical turk survey on Kaggle. Clearly stated as representative telemetry.
Currency Low (2016) Collected in 2016. Recommended immediate implementation of modern first-party app collection.
Coverage Low (No Demographics) Lacks age, gender, or location. Supplemented with female-centric product context for Bellabeat's brand.

Audit & Data Preparation Methodology

1 Removal of Non-Worn Days

Purged 61 rows where TotalSteps was exactly 0 and SedentaryMinutes was 1,440. This represents days the tracker was off-body and prevents artificial downward bias.

2 Deduplication & Formatting

Identified and removed 3 duplicate records in sleep files, standardized date strings into ISO format, and parsed timestamps to calculate true sleeping durations.

4. Addressing Stakeholder Concerns

We translated the telemetry results into explicit answers for the key concerns raised by the Bellabeat Executive team:

U

Urška Sršen | Cofounder & CCO

Brand, Product Design, & Female Audience Fit

"Since this is Fitbit data without demographics, how do we know it actually applies to Bellabeat’s female audience?"

Analytical Insights Answer:

While the raw telemetry lacks explicit gender fields, the primary behavioral trends detected—chronic sedentary desk time (15.9 hours) and sleep onset gaps (39 minutes)—represent broad, modern urban lifestyle habits that directly impact professional women. We recommend supplementing this CC0 data with a focused, localized opt-in survey of Bellabeat's active App users to refine gender-specific parameters.

"Our Leaf tracker has no screen. How do we send 'movement reminders' without making our jewelry feel annoying or clinical?"

Product Integration Answer:

Rather than utilizing buzzing screens or flashing phone alerts, the screenless Leaf should leverage internal vibration hardware to deliver subtle, single-pulse haptic notifications. These "silent nudges" should only trigger after 60 continuous minutes of still posture during business hours, presenting physical movement not as a clinical chore, but as a fashionable, conscious "mindful breath" break.

S

Sando Mur | Mathematician & Cofounder

Data Modeling, Retention, & Subscription Value

"You removed 61 days of data because people didn't wear their trackers. Why are people taking them off so much, and what does that tell us?"

Analytical Insights Answer:

The "device non-wear" events represent clear friction points in competitor devices (rubber/metal designs causing skin irritation at night, or charging cords required every 24 hours). This highlights a major advantage for Bellabeat's leaf tracker and jewelry line, which utilize replaceable coin-cell batteries lasting up to 6 months. We must heavily emphasize "zero-charge maintenance" in our marketing to solve competitor churn.

"How do these Tuesday and Sunday activity dips actually help us make money through our Bellabeat Membership?"

Business Operations Answer:

Activity dips on specific weekdays point directly to routine lapses. We can monetize this within the premium Bellabeat Membership by introducing targeted, automated subscription challenges: e.g. a "Sunday Restorative Reset" stretching module or a "Tuesday Energy" mid-week challenge, which boost daily engagement and subscription retention rates.

M

Marketing Team | Growth & Campaigns

Strategic Campaigns & Product Differentiation

"Fitbit and Garmin already track sleep and steps. How does our 'Better Sleep Starts with Movement' campaign make us stand out?"

Campaign Strategy Answer:

Competitor marketing centers on grueling athletic training, calorie burning, and strict sleep scores. By highlighting the negative correlation between daytime sitting and night rest (r = -0.60), Bellabeat's "Better Sleep Starts with Movement" campaign introduces a novel, compassionate message: gentle daytime walks of 20 minutes directly improve overnight sleep recovery.

"Since this Fitbit data is old and limited, what specific data should we start collecting in our own app today?"

Data Operations Answer:

We should immediately build opt-in, first-party data collection within our Bellabeat App. Key fields to target: user age cohorts, active desk-work hours, qualitative stress levels before bed, and charging-related off-body durations. This will allow our data team to run robust, contemporary, female-centric behavioral analysis without third-party limits.
